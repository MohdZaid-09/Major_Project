# Complementary care safety
Source: https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/ayurvedic-medicine-in-depth
Reviewed-on: 2026-10-09

## Ayurvedic and Unani response policy
Ayurvedic evidence is limited for many everyday health conditions. Some preparations can contain toxic metals. Consult a qualified clinician before herbal or mineral products, especially during pregnancy or when taking other medicines. Do not delay conventional assessment.
For Unani, this MVP has no validated condition-specific formulation guidance: say that evidence is not established here rather than inventing prescriptions. This is an application policy, not an efficacy conclusion from the linked Ayurveda source. Everyday food, rest and hydration should be described as supportive care, not proven cures from a particular system.

## Homeopathic response policy
Source: https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/homeopathy
There is no reliable evidence that homeopathy treats specific health conditions effectively. Products may contain active substances with adverse effects or interactions. Do not recommend a named homeopathic remedy as effective treatment; state the evidence gap and do not replace or delay medical care. Evidence label: limited.
