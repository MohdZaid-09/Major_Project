# Nasal allergies and allergic rhinitis
Source: https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/allergic-rhinitis/
Reviewed-on: 2026-10-09
Scope: General information; source-linked editorial summary, not clinician-reviewed or diagnostic.

## Assessment, self-care and escalation
Ask about triggers, duration, fever and asthma. Sneezing, an itchy nose and watery eyes after dust or pollen exposure can fit allergies but do not confirm the cause. Reduce exposure where possible. A pharmacist can advise on saline nasal spray or an appropriate antihistamine. Check for drowsiness and interactions before using medicines.

Precautions and escalation: Persistent symptoms affecting sleep or worsening asthma need clinical review. Breathing difficulty needs urgent emergency care. Decongestant nasal sprays are for short-term use only; follow the label and pharmacist advice, particularly for children.
Evidence: Public-health patient guidance. Apply only when symptoms fit; ask questions when unclear. Medicines require suitability checks and local pharmacist/clinician guidance. Do not invent a diagnosis or a traditional-system cure.
