# Common Cold

## Allopathic (Modern Medicine)
The common cold is a mild viral infection of the upper respiratory tract, usually self-limiting within 7-10 days. There is no cure; care focuses on symptom relief — rest, fluids, steam inhalation, and OTC options like decongestants or antihistamines as directed on packaging. Antibiotics are not effective against colds since they are viral, not bacterial. See a doctor if symptoms last beyond 10 days, worsen after initial improvement, or include high fever, chest pain, or difficulty breathing (which could indicate a secondary infection).
Evidence Level: strong
Precautions: Avoid unnecessary antibiotic use. Consult a doctor before giving cold medications to young children, as many are not recommended for children under a certain age.

## Ayurvedic
Ayurveda often links the common cold (Pratishyaya) to Kapha dosha imbalance and reduced Agni. Traditional remedies include steam inhalation with ajwain (carom seeds) or eucalyptus, warm turmeric milk (haldi doodh), ginger-tulsi tea, and avoiding cold/heavy foods during recovery.
Evidence Level: traditional/anecdotal
Precautions: Generally low-risk for common cold symptoms, but consult a doctor if symptoms are severe, prolonged, or occur in someone with an underlying respiratory condition like asthma.

## Unani
Unani medicine associates cold and catarrh (Nazla-Zukam) with excess phlegm (Balgham) affecting the respiratory temperament. Management often includes Ilaj-bil-Ghiza (warm, light diet), steam inhalation with medicinal herbs, and formulations intended to help clear phlegm, guided by a Hakim.
Evidence Level: traditional/anecdotal
Precautions: If symptoms persist beyond the typical course or worsen, medical evaluation is recommended rather than continued self-treatment.

## Homeopathic
Common homeopathic remedies considered for cold symptoms include Allium cepa (for watery eyes and runny nose) and Arsenicum album, selected based on specific symptom patterns. Evidence from controlled trials for meaningful benefit over placebo remains limited.
Evidence Level: limited
Precautions: Seek medical care if cold-like symptoms are accompanied by high fever, chest pain, or breathing difficulty, as these are not typical of an uncomplicated cold.
