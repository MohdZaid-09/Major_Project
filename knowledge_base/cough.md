# Cough

## Allopathic (Modern Medicine)
Cough is a reflex to clear the airway and is often caused by viral infection, postnasal drip, or irritants. Dry coughs and productive (phlegm-producing) coughs may be managed differently — honey (for adults and children over 1 year) can help soothe throat irritation, staying hydrated helps thin mucus, and OTC cough suppressants or expectorants may be used as directed on packaging for short-term relief. See a doctor if cough persists beyond 3 weeks, produces blood, or is accompanied by high fever, chest pain, or shortness of breath.
Evidence Level: strong
Precautions: Avoid giving OTC cough/cold medication to children under 4 without medical advice. Persistent cough (over 3 weeks) warrants medical evaluation to rule out conditions like asthma, infection, or other underlying causes.

## Ayurvedic
Ayurveda categorizes cough (Kasa) by dominant dosha involvement and often recommends warm herbal decoctions with tulsi, ginger, and honey, along with steam inhalation. Turmeric milk is a commonly used traditional remedy believed to soothe throat irritation.
Evidence Level: traditional/anecdotal
Precautions: Persistent, worsening, or blood-tinged cough should be medically evaluated rather than managed with home remedies alone.

## Unani
In Unani practice, cough (Su'aal) is often linked to respiratory Balgham (phlegm) imbalance, with management involving Ilaj-bil-Ghiza (diet suited to clearing phlegm), steam therapies, and Hakim-guided herbal formulations aimed at soothing the throat and airway.
Evidence Level: traditional/anecdotal
Precautions: A cough lasting more than a few weeks, or with any red-flag symptoms, should be assessed by a medical professional rather than continued traditional self-care.

## Homeopathic
Homeopathic remedies for cough are selected based on the cough's character (e.g. Bryonia for dry painful cough, Antimonium tartaricum for rattling chest cough), per homeopathic symptom-matching principles. High-quality evidence of benefit beyond placebo remains limited for most conditions including cough.
Evidence Level: limited
Precautions: Do not delay medical evaluation for a cough that is persistent, worsening, or accompanied by concerning symptoms like blood, high fever, or breathing difficulty.
