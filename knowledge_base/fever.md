# Fever

## Allopathic (Modern Medicine)
Fever is the body's natural immune response, usually triggered by an infection (viral or bacterial), and is not itself a disease but a symptom. Most fevers below 102°F (38.9°C) in adults do not require medication and resolve as the underlying cause is treated. Rest, fluids, and monitoring temperature are the first line of care. Over-the-counter antipyretics (like paracetamol/acetaminophen) can be used as directed on the packaging to improve comfort. A doctor should be consulted if fever exceeds 103°F (39.4°C), lasts more than 3 days, or is accompanied by severe headache, stiff neck, confusion, difficulty breathing, or a rash.
Evidence Level: strong
Precautions: Do not exceed the dosage listed on OTC medication packaging. Avoid combining multiple products containing the same active ingredient (e.g. paracetamol) to prevent overdose. Seek urgent care for very high fever, fever in infants under 3 months, or fever with any emergency symptoms.

## Ayurvedic
In Ayurveda, fever (Jwara) is considered to arise from an imbalance of the doshas, commonly linked to accumulated Ama (toxins) disturbing Agni (digestive fire). Traditional approaches include langhana (fasting or light diet), warm herbal decoctions such as tulsi (holy basil) and ginger tea, and rest. Some traditional texts describe use of herbs like Sudarshan Ghan Vati, though quality-controlled clinical evidence for these is limited.
Evidence Level: traditional/anecdotal
Precautions: Herbal preparations are not a substitute for medical evaluation of the underlying cause of fever. Consult a qualified Ayurvedic practitioner before combining herbal remedies with prescription medication, as interactions are possible.

## Unani
Unani medicine (Tibb) attributes fever (Humma) to an imbalance among the four Akhlat (humors: blood, phlegm, yellow bile, black bile), often linked to Su-e-Mizaj (temperament disturbance). Traditional management includes Ilaj-bil-Ghiza (dietary regulation, often light and cooling foods), Ilaj-bil-Tadbir (regimenal therapy such as rest and controlled environment), and use of cooling/diaphoretic herbal formulations under a Hakim's guidance.
Evidence Level: traditional/anecdotal
Precautions: Not a substitute for identifying and treating the underlying cause of fever, especially in cases of high or persistent fever. Seek a qualified Hakim or physician rather than self-medicating with unregulated formulations.

## Homeopathic
Homeopathic approaches to fever often involve remedies selected based on the specific symptom pattern (e.g. Belladonna for sudden high fever with flushed face, Aconite for fever with anxiety after cold exposure), following homeopathic principle of individualized symptom-matching. Robust clinical trial evidence supporting homeopathic remedies for fever over placebo is limited.
Evidence Level: limited
Precautions: Should not delay evaluation of fevers with concerning features (very high temperature, prolonged duration, or accompanying severe symptoms) by a conventional medical professional.
